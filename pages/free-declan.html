Incident Report

A Tampa Tragedy

What follows is a faithful, unsparing account of the events of that night, as reconstructed from the few brothers brave enough to come forward.

It was supposed to be a quiet weekend in Tampa. A change of scenery. A few drinks at a bar with a name nobody can quite remember now. Declan Coyne was, by all accounts, in fine form — well-dressed, well-mannered, and extremely well-prepared with a New Jersey driver's license that bore the wrong name and an unflattering haircut he did not have.

The bouncer disagreed. There was a discussion. Voices were raised. A wallet was, allegedly, dropped. And then a second wallet was, indisputably, also dropped — a development that even the most generous attorney would describe as "not ideal." Within minutes, Declan was in the back of a patrol car, wearing the same expression he wears on bid day photographs and freshman composites: handsome, mildly bewildered, and entirely innocent of whatever he is being accused of.

"He was just trying to get a drink. He was trying to live. They took him from us at last call."

By morning he had been processed, photographed, and placed in a holding cell with men who do not know what Lambda Chi Alpha is and who have not been briefed on his merits. The State of Florida set bail at five thousand dollars — a sum that, to a man on a college meal plan, is roughly the gross domestic product of a small island nation.

And so we come to you. His brothers. His chapter. His friends. The men who have watched him grow, and the men who have watched him try, and fail, repeatedly, to grow a mustache for Movember. We come to you because Declan cannot come to us. We come to you because every day Declan spends behind those walls is a day Zeta Rho is incomplete.

The Case

Why He Must Be Freed

There is a man-shaped hole in our chapter. The case for his immediate release is, frankly, overwhelming.

01

A Man of Character

Declan has never missed a chapter meeting on purpose. He has only ever missed them on accident, and afterwards he always feels really bad about it. That is the mark of a good man.

02

His Mother Is Asking

She has called four times. She is not yet aware of the situation. We would like to keep it that way. The fastest path to plausible deniability is the immediate physical return of her son.

03

Brotherhood Demands It

"Vir Quisque Vir" — Every Man a Man. The motto says nothing about leaving one of those men in a cinder-block room in Tampa. We checked. It is not in the constitution.

04

He Owes Us $40

For dues, for formal, for a bar tab in February that everyone has agreed to forget about. We cannot collect what is owed if the debtor is incarcerated. This is, in a sense, an investment.

05

Finals Are In Two Weeks

His professors do not accept "incarcerated in another county" as an excused absence. We have asked. They were specific about this. Time is, in the most literal sense, of the essence.

06

The Crescent Demands It

The crescent is our symbol — pure, high, ever growing. It is hard to grow when one of the points is being held without bond on Hillsborough Avenue. We owe him to the symbol.
